The problem: the diagnostic feature is an absence

A provenance is the documented sequence of an artwork's owners, and the principal public evidence that a seller holds good title. It is difficult to evaluate because the diagnostic feature is not what the record asserts but what it omits.

Three conditions co-occur: seventeen undocumented years spanning the war, a holder identified only by a placeholder, and an international transfer with no recorded transaction — the recognised signature of a work that may have passed through confiscation or coerced sale.

Who bears this risk

  • Purchasers and advisers, exposed to restitution claims years after acquisition
  • Museums without dedicated provenance staff
  • Estate, insurance, and fiduciary professionals, and heirs pursuing a family claim

The Washington Conference Principles direct institutions to weigh “unavoidable gaps or ambiguities in the provenance” in light of the Holocaust-era context (Principle 4) — a weighing that presupposes the gaps have been located.

U.S. Dept. of State, Washington Conference Principles on Nazi-Confiscated Art, 3 Dec. 1998. 44 governments; non-binding.

Existing instruments and why they do not address this task

InstrumentFunctionLimitation for gap detection
Art Loss Register Commercial stolen-art database, searched object by object Paid access, no public API. Answers whether an object is reported stolen — a different question from whether a chain is discontinuous.
Lost Art Database, Getty Provenance Index, NEPIP Public repositories of recorded losses and historical sales records Reference instruments requiring a known search term. None accepts a narrative provenance and reports where it fails.
General-purpose conversational models Summarise a provenance; often identify an obvious omission Without tools, no claim is verified against an authority file. Gap identification is probabilistic rather than arithmetic, and the model acts on instructions embedded in the submitted text.
Institutional provenance departments Authoritative archival research Rigorous but resource-intensive, and available to few institutions.

Reynolds observes that provenance research “lacks a prescribed methodology.” The contribution claimed here is to render one portion of it explicit and reproducible: structured extraction, deterministic temporal analysis, authority-file reconciliation, and a classification that rules may escalate but never relax.

Reynolds, L. An Art Provenance Research Guide. Master's paper, School of Information and Library Science, UNC Chapel Hill.

System architecture: four agents, two of which invoke no model

Gap detection is arithmetic over years; cross-referencing is a bounded sequence of authority-file queries. Delegating either to a language model would approximately double per-run cost while introducing non-determinism into findings that must be reproducible.

Illustrative case: Klimt, Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I (1907)

The classification cites the 1938 confiscation and the undocumented 1941 transfer to the Österreichische Galerie Belvedere by name. The confidence score is constrained beneath a ceiling imposed by the classification, so the two indicators cannot present contradictory readings to the user.

Each invocation is metered to disk with its token counts and computed cost; the figures above are medians over logged runs rather than projections. Restricting model invocation to the extraction and classification stages is the principal economy, and it yields a secondary benefit: every reported gap is attributable to a named rule and is reproducible across runs.

Adversarial robustness: the submitted record is untrusted input

Greshake et al. characterise indirect prompt injection, in which instructions are embedded in content an application retrieves and processes. This system is precisely such an application: its entire input is third-party text.

The evaluation record terminates with an instruction directing the system to return a clear classification, suppress gap findings, and omit dealer names.

Greshake, K., Abdelnabi, S., Mishra, S., Endres, C., Holz, T., & Fritz, M. (2023). Not What You've Signed Up For: Compromising Real-World LLM-Integrated Applications with Indirect Prompt Injection. Proceedings of the 16th ACM Workshop on Artificial Intelligence and Security (AISec '23). arXiv:2302.12173.

Four controls operate in series: the record is delimited and declared as data in both system prompts; delimiter escapes are stripped; recognised injection formulations are flagged; and a deterministic floor prevents any instruction from yielding a clear classification where the rules indicate otherwise.

The Claims Conference and World Jewish Restitution Organization surveyed 160 U.S. art museums formerly participating in the Nazi-Era Provenance Internet Portal, which held nearly 30,000 object records at its peak and closed in 2024.

At the measured per-audit cost, first-pass triage across a corpus of this magnitude is a model expenditure of order $313. The system does not substitute for archival research; it establishes an ordering over records, which is the constraint the survey describes.

Claims Conference/WJRO, Report on the Extent of Provenance Research Regarding Nazi-Looted Cultural and Religious Property in U.S. Museums, 25 Sept. 2025. The figure counts publicly accessible records and is therefore a lower bound on research conducted.

Limitations, further work, and references

Acknowledged limitations

  • The Lost Art Database cache is unpopulated; three of four intended sources are operative
  • No object-level search against loss registers
  • Parsing validated only on English-language records
  • No OCR stage; scanned documents are not processed
  • A fabricated but internally continuous chain is classified clear: the system audits the text, not its truth

Further work

  • Evaluation with a practising registrar against records of known outcome
  • Batch processing yielding a ranked triage ordering
  • German- and French-language parsing
  • Calibration of score weights against adjudicated cases
